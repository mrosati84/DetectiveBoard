Murder mystery detective board

The classic. Whether you’re hosting a murder mystery party, reading a whodunit or writing one, this layout puts every suspect next to the evidence that implicates them.

What to pin

  • One card per suspect, with a photo, motive and alibi.
  • Physical evidence: the weapon, the letter, the missing key.
  • Places and times, so alibis can be checked against each other.

Tip: tie every suspect to the scene with red string. The one you can’t connect is either innocent or very clever.

True crime case board

Following a podcast, a documentary or an unsolved case? An evidence board keeps the people, places and timeline in one view, so new details slot into place instead of getting lost in your notes.

What to pin

  • The victim and every person of interest.
  • A timeline card for the critical hours.
  • Locations, sightings and the sources you got them from.

Tip: write the source on each card. When two sources disagree, that’s where to dig.

Novel plot board

Writers have used cork boards for plotting long before detectives became famous for them. Lay out characters, secrets and reveals, then follow the red string to check that every twist is earned.

What to pin

  • Main characters, with what they want and what they hide.
  • Key clues and the chapter where each one appears.
  • Red herrings, with a note on when they get resolved.

Tip: a clue with no string leading to it is a twist your reader won’t believe.

Tabletop RPG campaign board

Game masters juggle factions, NPCs and secrets across months of sessions. A conspiracy board shows who knows what — and your players will love seeing it if you share a read-only link.

What to pin

  • Factions and major NPCs, with their goals.
  • Maps and locations the party has discovered.
  • One card per session with its open threads.

Tip: keep a private board for secrets and share a second one with your players.

How to use these templates

These detective board examples are starting points, not rules. Open a free board, add a card for each item on the “what to pin” list, then connect related cards with red string. Move things around as your theory changes — that’s the whole point of a pin board.
